If you have an old TV, broken monitor, or a drawer full of dead printers and you live in Hawaii, you cannot just bag them up and put them on the curb. Hawaii law restricts how electronics are disposed of, and throwing e-waste in regular trash can result in fines. Here is what you need to know.

## The Short Answer

No. Most electronics cannot go in your regular trash or blue bin on Oahu. Televisions, monitors, computers, laptops, printers, and many other devices are classified as covered electronic devices (CEDs) under Hawaii law and must be recycled through approved channels.

## What Hawaii Law Says (Act 162)

Hawaii Act 162, passed in 2008 and updated since, established the Electronic Device Recycling Program. Under this law:

- Manufacturers of covered electronic devices must fund collection and recycling programs in Hawaii.
- Consumers can drop off qualifying electronics at designated collection sites at no cost.
- Covered devices include televisions (all types — CRT, LCD, plasma, LED), computer monitors, laptops, desktop computers, and portable computers.
- Retailers selling covered devices must inform consumers about recycling options.

The law exists because electronics contain hazardous materials — lead, mercury, cadmium, and flame retardants — that contaminate soil and groundwater when buried in a landfill. On an island with limited land and a water table close to the surface, that contamination is a serious problem.

## What Counts as E-Waste

If it has a circuit board, a screen, or a battery, treat it as e-waste. Common items include:

- **Televisions** — CRT, flatscreen, plasma, LCD, LED, all types
- **Computer monitors and displays**
- **Desktop and laptop computers**
- **Printers, scanners, and fax machines**
- **Tablets and e-readers**
- **Cell phones**
- **Stereos and audio equipment**
- **Game consoles**
- **Small appliances with circuit boards** (routers, modems, external drives)

Small batteries (AA, AAA, lithium-ion) should also be recycled separately. Do not put them in regular trash — they can cause fires in garbage trucks and at H-POWER.

## Where to Drop Off Electronics on Oahu

Several free or low-cost options exist for Oahu residents:

- **City and County of Honolulu Transfer Stations** — Most accept limited quantities of electronics during regular hours. Check the [City ENV website](https://www.honolulu.gov/env) for current locations and accepted items.
- **Best Buy** — Accepts TVs (up to 32 inches), monitors, computers, printers, cables, and small electronics for recycling at no charge. Larger TVs may have a small fee.
- **Habitat for Humanity ReStore (Kalihi)** — Accepts working electronics in good condition as donations.
- **E-Cycling Hawaii events** — Periodic free collection events run by the state. Check the Hawaii Department of Health for upcoming dates.

These options work well if you have a few items and a vehicle to transport them.

## When a Removal Service Makes More Sense

Drop-off works for a printer or a small TV. It does not work for:

- **Estate cleanouts** where you are clearing out a houseful of old electronics
- **Office cleanouts** with dozens of monitors, CPUs, and printers
- **Large or heavy items** like 65-inch TVs, projection units, or old CRT televisions that weigh 100+ pounds
- **Condo residents** without a vehicle or easy access to a transfer station
- **Mixed loads** where electronics are part of a bigger junk removal job (furniture, appliances, general household items)

In these situations, scheduling a single pickup that handles everything — electronics included — saves time and multiple trips across the island.
