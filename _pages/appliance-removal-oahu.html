The Problem with Old Appliances

Getting rid of a large appliance is not as simple as dragging it to the curb. A refrigerator full of old freon, a washer stuck behind a bathroom door in a third-floor condo, or a water heater wedged into a utility closet — these are real obstacles that most people are not equipped to handle on their own. Moving heavy appliances through hallways and down stairs without damaging walls or floors takes experience and the right equipment. Then there is the question of what happens to the unit after it leaves your property.

Hawaii has its own disposal rules. Appliances containing refrigerant — refrigerators, chest freezers, window AC units — cannot go to the landfill or transfer station without proper freon recovery first. Electronics-adjacent items like dehumidifiers fall under Hawaii's e-waste guidelines. We handle the logistics so you do not have to figure it out yourself.

How We Handle Disposal

Not everything goes to the same place. Here is how we approach it:

  • Refrigerant recovery: Fridges, freezers, and AC units that still contain freon go through Refrigerant Management Company or a certified recovery partner before disposal. This is required under EPA Section 608 and Hawaii state rules. If your unit requires this step, we will let you know before pickup and include any associated fees in your quote.
  • Metal recycling: Washers, dryers, stoves, and water heaters are largely steel and aluminum. We route these to metal recyclers on Oahu where they are processed rather than buried.
  • E-waste compliance: Dehumidifiers and some other appliances fall under Hawaii's electronics disposal rules. We handle these through compliant channels. For TVs, computers, and other electronics, see our e-waste removal page.
  • General waste: Items that cannot be recycled or donated are processed through H-POWER, Oahu's waste-to-energy facility, keeping them out of the Waimanalo Gulch landfill.

When This Is Not the Right Option

If your appliance still works and is less than 10 years old, consider selling it on Facebook Marketplace before calling us. A functioning washer or refrigerator in decent condition can move quickly and put money back in your pocket. Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Kalihi accepts working appliances in good condition for donation. We are here for the units that are past their useful life, broken beyond repair, or simply unwanted — not to haul away something someone else could use.

What to Expect on Pickup Day

We prefer appliances placed outside — in the driveway, on a covered lanai, or near the garage entrance — to avoid interior wall and floor damage during the move. If the unit cannot be moved outside, that is fine. Let us know when you book so we can bring the right tools and protective materials. We will not assume it is outside if you have not told us.

On the day of pickup, we confirm your appointment window, arrive on time, load the unit, and clear out. No debris left behind, no unannounced crew changes. If access requires elevator coordination in a condo building, give us that information upfront and we will work around it.
