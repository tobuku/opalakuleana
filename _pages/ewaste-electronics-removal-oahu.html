Hawaii Has Strict Rules About Electronics Disposal — and Most People Don't Know It

On the mainland, tossing an old computer or TV into the dumpster is common practice. In Hawaii, it is illegal. Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 339D — the Hawaii Electronic Device Recycling Act — specifically prohibits disposing of covered electronic devices in solid waste landfills. That includes televisions, monitors, desktop computers, laptops, and more. The law exists because these devices contain hazardous materials: lead in CRT picture tubes, mercury in LCD backlights, cadmium in batteries, and flame retardants in circuit boards.

CRT televisions are especially problematic. A single CRT TV can contain up to four pounds of lead. They cannot go to H-POWER, cannot go to the landfill, and are not accepted at Best Buy or Goodwill. Getting rid of them legally requires a certified e-waste recycler — and getting them there requires someone to move them.

What HRS 339D Means for You

Hawaii's Electronic Device Recycling Act places the recycling obligation on manufacturers, but the practical reality is that getting your old devices to a certified recycler falls on you. The City and County of Honolulu does hold periodic free e-waste collection events, and some retailers like Best Buy offer limited drop-off for small devices. However, these options have real limits:

  • City e-waste events are periodic and require you to load and transport the items yourself
  • Best Buy accepts small electronics but does not accept CRT monitors or CRT televisions
  • Goodwill accepts working electronics but does not accept CRTs and turns away items in poor condition

When volume, item type, or logistics make self-haul impractical, we step in. We know which certified recyclers on Oahu are equipped for CRTs and hazardous electronics, and we handle the transport.

When This Is Not the Right Option

If you have a single laptop, a smartphone, or a small box of cables, hiring a junk removal company is overkill. Better options for small quantities:

  • City & County of Honolulu e-waste events — held periodically at locations around Oahu, free to residents
  • Best Buy drop-off — accepts most small electronics for recycling at no charge (excludes CRTs)
  • Manufacturer take-back programs — Apple, Dell, and others offer mail-in or drop-off recycling for their own devices

Where we make sense: you have multiple items, large or heavy equipment, CRTs that nowhere else will take, or you are doing a larger cleanout and the electronics are part of it. See our commercial cleanout and storage unit cleanout pages for related services.
