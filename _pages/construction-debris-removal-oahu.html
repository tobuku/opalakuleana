Renovations Generate Debris Fast — and Getting Rid of It Is Its Own Problem

A kitchen gut, a bathroom remodel, a roof replacement — every renovation creates a pile of material that has nowhere to go. Lumber, drywall, tile, concrete, old fixtures, and scrap metal accumulate quickly, and on Oahu there is no simple way to dispose of it yourself. The landfill at PVT Land Company's Waimanalo Gulch facility accepts construction and demolition debris, but hauling there requires a vehicle large enough for the load and knowledge of what each facility will and will not accept.

Contractors sometimes leave debris behind when the job scope does not include cleanup. DIY homeowners often get halfway through a project and realize they have no plan for removal. Either way, Opala Kuleana handles it. We load, haul, and route your construction debris to the right facilities — including metal recycling for scrap metal, H-POWER for eligible combustible waste, and Waimanalo Gulch for everything else.

What We Remove

We haul all types of construction and renovation debris, including:

  • Lumber and framing wood
  • Drywall and sheetrock
  • Tile and ceramic fragments
  • Concrete chunks and masonry
  • Scrap metal (pipe, rebar, copper, steel)
  • Old flooring (carpet, vinyl, hardwood, laminate)
  • Insulation
  • Old cabinets and vanities
  • Countertops
  • Roofing materials (shingles, underlayment)
  • Plumbing fixtures (sinks, toilets, tubs)

Mixed loads are common. A bathroom remodel typically produces drywall, tile, a vanity, a tub or shower unit, and plumbing fixtures all at once. We handle the full mix in a single trip rather than making you sort it yourself.

Pricing: Volume and Weight Both Matter

Construction debris is priced differently from household junk. Standard junk removal is typically priced by volume — how much truck space your items take up. Construction debris adds a weight component.

Concrete, masonry, rock, and tile are dense. A quarter-truckload of broken concrete can weigh as much as a full truck of lumber and drywall. Disposal facilities charge by weight for heavy material, and that cost passes through to the job. When you call for a quote, let us know what your load contains. A debris pile that is mostly lumber and drywall is priced differently than one that is mostly concrete and tile.

We give you a firm price before we start — no surprises when the job is done.

Where Construction Debris Goes on Oahu

Oahu has specific facilities for different material types, and routing matters for both cost and compliance:

  • PVT Land Company — Waimanalo Gulch: The primary construction and demolition debris landfill on Oahu. Accepts mixed C&D loads including concrete, drywall, lumber, and tile.
  • H-POWER: The City and County of Honolulu's waste-to-energy plant accepts eligible combustible waste, including untreated lumber and certain other materials. Diverting appropriate material here keeps it out of the landfill.
  • Metal recycling facilities: Scrap metal from renovations — copper pipe, steel framing, cast iron, aluminum — goes to metal recyclers on Oahu rather than the landfill. This is better for the environment and sometimes offsets disposal cost on metal-heavy loads.

You do not need to know which facility handles what. We sort and route the material on our end.

When This Is Not the Right Option

For large-scale demolition projects generating multiple truckloads of debris daily — full structure teardowns, major commercial renovations, ongoing construction sites — a roll-off dumpster rental is likely more cost-effective. A dumpster rental allows you to load at your own pace over several days without scheduling multiple pickups.

Opala Kuleana is also not a demolition contractor. We remove debris after the work is done. If you need walls knocked down, concrete broken up, or structures dismantled, hire a licensed demolition contractor first. Once the work is finished and the material is in a pile, that is when we come in.

Where we make the most sense: a single-room or partial renovation, a project that generated more debris than expected, or a job where the contractor left the cleanup unfinished.
